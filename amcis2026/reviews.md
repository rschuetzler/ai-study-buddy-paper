# Reviews

## Minitrack chair
This is an interesting experiment exploring an HCI perspective. I look forward to
seeing how this paper develops. Please refer to the reviewers’
comments for further details, particularly regarding the justification
for allowing participants to self-select their study materials, which
remains slightly unconvincing.


## R1

This study is going to explore the role of embodiment and social cues in shaping
academic productivity behaviours. The integration of AI and robotics
as a research focus is particularly timely and emerging, and the study
stands to make a meaningful contribution to IS education.

The authors demonstrate a solid understanding of the current landscape in both AI
and human-robot interaction. The literature review is generally well-
structured and relevant. The theoretical framing is appropriately
selected. The hypotheses are clearly stated and logically derived from
the theoretical discussion. Yet, given that the central focus of this
paper is on how different forms of AI presence influence students'
focus, productivity, and engagement during self-study, it seems a
notable omission that no hypothesis directly addresses the
relationship between AI embodiment and learning-related outcomes. This
risks a misalignment between the stated research focus and the formal
hypothesis structure. The authors are encouraged to consider adding a
hypothesis that more explicitly connects embodiment to productivity or
engagement outcomes.

The overall research design is reasonable and appropriate for an ERF paper.
However, the justification for allowing participants to self-select
their study materials remains not fully convincing. While the authors
acknowledge the variability this introduces, a more explicit
explanation of how this variability will be managed or controlled in
the analysis would strengthen confidence in the design. Additionally,
the data analysis section is quite brief. It would be appreciated if
the authors could at least identify the specific scales or instruments
that will be used to measure perceived productivity, concentration,
and related outcomes, so that readers can better evaluate the
construct validity of the proposed measures.


## R2

Thank you to the author(s) for submitting this manuscript to the conference.

This study examines how different forms of AI presence influence university
students’ focus, productivity, and engagement during a self‑study
session. Grounded in social presence theory and the concept of body
doubling, the author(s) conduct an experiment comparing four
conditions: (1) AI chatbot on the participant’s personal laptop; (2)
AI chatbot on a research‑provided laptop; (3) Embodied AI; and (4) No
AI assistance. The topic is timely and highly relevant to IS
education, and the manuscript is clearly written and well‑motivated. I
hope the following comments help strengthen the work.

#1 Both social presence theory and the body‑doubling concept are appropriate
theoretical foundations; however, in the self‑reported measures
section, it is unclear whether the survey measures fully capture the
key constructs in the hypotheses, particularly the social presence,
and body‑doubling effect. Based on the descriptions provided, the
measures appear to be influenced more by Technology Acceptance Model
(TAM) constructs than by social presence or body‑doubling constructs.
If page limits allow, the authors should provide additional
justification demonstrating that the selected measures adequately
reflect the theorized constructs.

#2 The experiment allows students to “select their own study materials to enable
more authentic and natural self‑study behaviors.” While this choice
may enhance validity, it would be helpful for the authors to justify
why this design is preferable to requiring participants to study the
same or similar materials. Without explanation, variability in study
content could introduce confounds that make it harder to interpret
differences across conditions.

#3 If page limits allow, it would strengthen the paper to include a table
summarizing and comparing the three AI conditions (personal laptop
chatbot, research‑provided laptop chatbot, and embodied AI). Such a
table would allow readers to easily understand the key differences in
functionality, modality, interaction style, and system capabilities
across conditions.

#4 The first instance of ADHD should be spelled out to ensure clarity for all
readers.
